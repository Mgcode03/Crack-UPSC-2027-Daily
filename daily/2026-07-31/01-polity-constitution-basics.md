# Day 1 Part 1: Indian Polity – Constitution Basics (Read Time ~70-80 min)

## Why Constitution Matters for UPSC
The Constitution of India is the supreme law. Almost every year 12-20 questions in Prelims come from Polity, and GS-II in Mains is heavily based on it. Understanding the foundational concepts gives you an edge in both objective and descriptive answers.

## Historical Underpinnings
The idea of a written constitution for India grew during the freedom struggle. The Cabinet Mission Plan of 1946 proposed a Constituent Assembly. The Assembly was formed under the Cabinet Mission. Dr. Rajendra Prasad was elected President of the Constituent Assembly. Dr. B.R. Ambedkar was the Chairman of the Drafting Committee. The Assembly took 2 years 11 months and 18 days to complete the work. The Constitution was adopted on 26 November 1949 and came into force on 26 January 1950 (Republic Day). 26 January was chosen because on that day in 1930 the Indian National Congress had declared Poorna Swaraj.

Key fact to memorise: The original Constitution had 395 Articles in 22 Parts and 8 Schedules. Today it has 470 Articles (approx., after amendments), 25 Parts and 12 Schedules.

## Sources of the Indian Constitution
India borrowed several features from other countries, adapted to Indian conditions:

- **United Kingdom**: Parliamentary form of government, Rule of Law, Cabinet system, Prerogative writs, Bicameralism, Single citizenship.
- **United States**: Fundamental Rights, Judicial Review, Independent Judiciary, Impeachment of President, Removal of Supreme Court and High Court judges, Preamble concepts of liberty and justice.
- **Ireland**: Directive Principles of State Policy, Method of election of President, Nomination of members to Rajya Sabha.
- **Canada**: Federation with a strong Centre, Residuary powers with Centre, Appointment of State Governors by Centre, Advisory jurisdiction of Supreme Court.
- **Australia**: Concurrent List, Freedom of trade and commerce, Joint sitting of Parliament.
- **Germany (Weimar)**: Suspension of Fundamental Rights during Emergency.
- **Soviet Union (USSR)**: Fundamental Duties, Ideal of justice (social, economic, political) in Preamble.
- **France**: Republic, Ideals of Liberty, Equality, Fraternity in Preamble.
- **South Africa**: Procedure for amendment of Constitution, Election of members of Rajya Sabha.
- **Japan**: Procedure established by law.

Memorise at least the major ones: UK (Parliamentary), USA (FRs + Judicial Review), Ireland (DPSPs), Canada (Strong Centre).

## Preamble
The Preamble is the introductory statement. It is not enforceable in court but is part of the Constitution (Kesavananda Bharati case, 1973). It declares India as a Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic Republic and secures Justice (social, economic, political), Liberty (of thought, expression, belief, faith and worship), Equality (of status and opportunity), and Fraternity (assuring dignity of individual and unity and integrity of the nation).

The words Socialist and Secular were added by the 42nd Amendment Act, 1976. The original Preamble did not have them.

Key phrase: “We, the people of India… do hereby adopt, enact and give to ourselves this Constitution.”

## Nature of the Indian Constitution – Federal or Unitary?
India is a federal system with unitary bias. Features of federalism: dual polity (Centre and States), written Constitution, supremacy of Constitution, rigid Constitution (amendment procedure), independent judiciary, bicameralism, division of powers.

Unitary features: Strong Centre (more subjects in Union List, residuary powers with Centre), single Constitution, single citizenship, integrated judiciary, appointment of Governors by Centre, All-India Services, Emergency provisions that convert federation into unitary system.

Supreme Court in S.R. Bommai case (1994) held that federalism is part of the basic structure.

## Basic Structure Doctrine
In Kesavananda Bharati vs State of Kerala (1973), the Supreme Court held that Parliament can amend any part of the Constitution under Article 368 but cannot alter the “basic structure”. What constitutes basic structure has been expanded over time through various judgments: supremacy of Constitution, republican and democratic form of government, secular character, separation of powers, federal character, judicial review, free and fair elections, Rule of Law, independence of judiciary, etc.

This doctrine is unique to India and is a powerful check on parliamentary power.

## Parts of the Constitution (High Frequency)
- Part I: The Union and its Territory (Articles 1-4)
- Part II: Citizenship (5-11)
- Part III: Fundamental Rights (12-35) – most important for Prelims and Mains
- Part IV: Directive Principles of State Policy (36-51)
- Part IVA: Fundamental Duties (51A) – added by 42nd Amendment
- Part V: The Union (52-151) – President, Vice-President, Council of Ministers, Parliament, etc.
- Part VI: The States (152-237)
- Part XI: Relations between Union and States (245-263)
- Part XV: Elections (324-329A)
- Part XVIII: Emergency Provisions (352-360)
- Part XX: Amendment of the Constitution (368)

Memorise the Part numbers for Fundamental Rights, DPSPs, Duties, Emergency, Amendment.

## Schedules (Important Ones)
1. First Schedule: Names of States and Union Territories
2. Second Schedule: Salaries and allowances of constitutional functionaries
3. Third Schedule: Forms of Oaths and Affirmations
4. Fourth Schedule: Allocation of seats in Rajya Sabha
5. Fifth Schedule: Administration of Scheduled Areas and Scheduled Tribes
6. Sixth Schedule: Administration of Tribal Areas in Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura, Mizoram
7. Seventh Schedule: Union List, State List, Concurrent List (most important – distribution of legislative powers)
8. Eighth Schedule: Official Languages (22 languages currently)
9. Ninth Schedule: Acts and Regulations protected from judicial review (added by 1st Amendment)
10. Tenth Schedule: Anti-Defection Law (added by 52nd Amendment)
11. Eleventh Schedule: Panchayats (73rd Amendment)
12. Twelfth Schedule: Municipalities (74th Amendment)

Focus on 7th, 8th, 10th, 11th, 12th for now.

## Key Takeaways to Memorise Today
1. Constitution adopted 26 Nov 1949, enforced 26 Jan 1950.
2. Drafting Committee Chairman: Dr. B.R. Ambedkar.
3. Basic Structure: Kesavananda Bharati 1973.
4. Preamble is part of Constitution but not enforceable.
5. Federal with strong unitary bias.
6. Seventh Schedule distributes powers.
7. 42nd Amendment is the most comprehensive (Mini Constitution).
