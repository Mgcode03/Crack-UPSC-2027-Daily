# Day 1 Part 4: Indian Economy Fundamentals (Read Time ~60 min)

## Why Economy
Economy questions (12-18 in Prelims) are conceptual + current. GS-III in Mains is economy-heavy. Start with basic concepts that appear repeatedly.

## National Income Concepts
Gross Domestic Product (GDP): Total value of all final goods and services produced within the domestic territory of a country in a year. Measured at market prices or factor cost.

Gross National Product (GNP): GDP + Net Factor Income from Abroad (NFIA). NFIA = income earned by residents abroad minus income earned by foreigners in the country.

Net Domestic Product (NDP) = GDP – Depreciation.
Net National Product (NNP) = GNP – Depreciation. NNP at factor cost is National Income.

Per Capita Income = National Income / Population.

India now largely uses GDP at market prices. Base year is revised periodically (currently 2011-12; new series expected).

## Sectors of Economy
Primary: Agriculture, mining, fishing, forestry – extraction of natural resources.
Secondary: Manufacturing, construction, electricity – processing.
Tertiary: Services – trade, transport, banking, IT, tourism, government services.

In India, tertiary sector contributes the largest share to GDP, followed by secondary, then primary. But primary still employs the largest workforce (structural issue).

## Inflation
Sustained rise in general price level. Measured by Consumer Price Index (CPI) and Wholesale Price Index (WPI). RBI uses CPI for monetary policy targeting (currently 4% ± 2%).

Types: Demand-pull (excess demand), Cost-push (rise in input costs), Built-in (wage-price spiral).

Effects: Reduces purchasing power, hurts fixed income groups, increases inequality, creates uncertainty for investment.

Deflation: Fall in prices – can lead to recession if demand collapses.

Stagflation: High inflation + high unemployment + stagnant growth.

## Monetary Policy
Controlled by Reserve Bank of India (RBI). Tools:

Quantitative: Repo Rate (rate at which RBI lends to banks against securities), Reverse Repo, Cash Reserve Ratio (CRR – % of deposits banks keep with RBI), Statutory Liquidity Ratio (SLR – % of deposits in liquid assets), Open Market Operations (buy/sell government securities).

Qualitative: Credit rationing, moral suasion, margin requirements.

Current framework: Flexible Inflation Targeting since 2016. Monetary Policy Committee (MPC) decides rates. Six members – 3 from RBI, 3 external.

## Fiscal Policy
Government’s revenue and expenditure policy. Budget is the main instrument.

Revenue Receipts: Tax (direct – income tax, corporate tax; indirect – GST, customs, excise) + Non-tax (fees, fines, dividends).

Capital Receipts: Borrowings, recovery of loans, disinvestment.

Revenue Expenditure: Consumption – salaries, interest payments, subsidies (does not create assets).
Capital Expenditure: Creates assets – infrastructure, loans given.

Fiscal Deficit = Total Expenditure – (Revenue Receipts + Non-debt Capital Receipts). It shows borrowing requirement.
Primary Deficit = Fiscal Deficit – Interest Payments.
Revenue Deficit = Revenue Expenditure – Revenue Receipts.

FRBM Act aims to control deficits.

## Banking Basics
Commercial banks accept deposits and give loans. They create credit.

Types of deposits: Current, Savings, Fixed, Recurring.

Priority Sector Lending: Banks must lend a prescribed % to agriculture, MSME, education, housing, etc.

NPA (Non-Performing Asset): Loan on which interest or principal is overdue for more than 90 days.

RBI is the central bank – banker to government, banker to banks, issuer of currency, regulator, manager of foreign exchange.

## Poverty and Unemployment Concepts
Poverty: Absolute (inability to meet basic needs) vs Relative. India uses Tendulkar, Rangarajan committees historically; now multidimensional poverty index also used.

Unemployment: Open, disguised (more people than needed in agriculture), seasonal, structural, frictional, cyclical.

Labour Force Participation Rate, Worker Population Ratio, Unemployment Rate – key indicators from PLFS (Periodic Labour Force Survey).

## Key Facts to Memorise
- RBI established 1935, nationalised 1949.
- First Five Year Plan 1951-56 (Harrod-Domar model focus).
- LPG reforms 1991 – Liberalisation, Privatisation, Globalisation under PV Narasimha Rao and Manmohan Singh.
- GST introduced 1 July 2017 – one nation one tax.
- Current inflation target 4% with tolerance band of ±2%.
