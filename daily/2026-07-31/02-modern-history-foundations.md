# Day 1 Part 2: Modern Indian History – Foundations (Read Time ~70 min)

## Why Modern History is Crucial
Modern Indian History (from mid-18th century) is a high-weightage area for both Prelims and Mains GS-I. Freedom Struggle questions appear every year. Understanding the sequence of events, personalities, and movements helps in writing analytical answers and eliminating options in MCQs.

## Advent of Europeans
The Portuguese were the first to arrive. Vasco da Gama reached Calicut in 1498. They established trading posts at Goa, Daman, Diu. Alfonso de Albuquerque captured Goa in 1510. Portuguese power declined after the arrival of other Europeans.

The Dutch (Netherlands) established the Dutch East India Company in 1602. They had posts at Masulipatnam, Pulicat, Surat, Chinsura. They were defeated by the English in the Battle of Bedara (1759).

The English East India Company was formed in 1600 by a royal charter of Queen Elizabeth I. Their first factory was at Surat (1613). In 1639 they got Madras (Fort St. George). In 1661 Bombay was received as dowry from Portuguese. In 1690 they founded Calcutta (Fort William).

The French East India Company was founded in 1664. Their main centres were Pondicherry, Chandernagore, Mahe, Karaikal, Yanam. Anglo-French rivalry culminated in the Carnatic Wars.

## Carnatic Wars (1746-1763)
These were fought in South India between English and French, with Indian rulers as allies.

First Carnatic War (1746-48): Linked to War of Austrian Succession in Europe. French captured Madras. Treaty of Aix-la-Chapelle restored Madras to English.

Second Carnatic War (1749-54): Succession disputes in Hyderabad and Carnatic. Robert Clive’s success at Arcot (1751) was decisive. French influence declined.

Third Carnatic War (1756-63): Linked to Seven Years’ War. Battle of Wandiwash (1760) – English under Eyre Coote defeated French. Treaty of Paris (1763) ended French political power in India; they retained only trading posts.

Key personality: Robert Clive – laid foundation of British Empire in India.

## Battle of Plassey (1757)
This is the turning point. Siraj-ud-Daulah was the Nawab of Bengal. The English under Robert Clive defeated him on 23 June 1757 at Plassey. Reasons: misuse of dastaks (trade permits), fortification of Calcutta, black hole incident (exaggerated), conspiracy with Mir Jafar, Jagat Seth, Omichund.

Result: Mir Jafar became Nawab. English got Zamindari of 24 Parganas and huge wealth. Dual system of government started later under Clive (Company collected revenue, Nawab handled administration).

## Battle of Buxar (1764)
Fought on 22 October 1764. English under Hector Munro defeated the combined forces of Mir Qasim (Bengal), Shuja-ud-Daulah (Awadh) and Shah Alam II (Mughal Emperor).

Treaty of Allahabad (1765): Shah Alam II granted Diwani (revenue collection rights) of Bengal, Bihar and Orissa to the Company. This gave the Company legal authority over the richest province of India. Dual government formalised.

## Expansion under Warren Hastings and Cornwallis
Warren Hastings (1773-1785): First Governor-General of Bengal under Regulating Act 1773. Rohilla War, First Anglo-Maratha War, Second Anglo-Mysore War. He was impeached later but acquitted.

Lord Cornwallis (1786-1793): Introduced Permanent Settlement in Bengal (1793) – fixed land revenue with zamindars. Reformed judiciary (Cornwallis Code). Third Anglo-Mysore War – Tipu Sultan defeated, Treaty of Seringapatam (1792) – Tipu ceded half territory and paid indemnity.

## Tipu Sultan and Mysore Wars
Haidar Ali rose in Mysore. First Anglo-Mysore War (1767-69) – Treaty of Madras. Second (1780-84) – Treaty of Mangalore. Third (1790-92) under Cornwallis. Fourth (1799) under Wellesley – Tipu killed, Mysore under Wodeyar with subsidiary alliance.

Tipu was a moderniser – rocket technology, trade with French, attempted industrialisation.

## Subsidiary Alliance (Lord Wellesley 1798-1805)
Indian rulers accepting subsidiary alliance had to: disband their army, accept British troops for protection (paid by the ruler), accept a British Resident, not enter alliances with other powers without British permission. States that accepted: Hyderabad, Mysore, Awadh, Tanjore, etc. This expanded British control without direct annexation.

## Doctrine of Lapse (Lord Dalhousie 1848-1856)
If a ruler died without a natural male heir, the state lapsed to the British. Applied to Satara, Jhansi, Nagpur, Sambalpur, Udaipur, etc. Annexation of Awadh (1856) on grounds of misgovernance also happened under him. These policies created deep resentment leading to 1857.

## Key Timelines to Memorise
- 1498: Vasco da Gama
- 1600: English EIC
- 1757: Plassey
- 1764: Buxar
- 1765: Diwani of Bengal
- 1793: Permanent Settlement
- 1799: Tipu’s death
- 1848-56: Dalhousie – Doctrine of Lapse

## Personality Focus
Robert Clive, Warren Hastings, Cornwallis, Wellesley, Dalhousie, Tipu Sultan, Haidar Ali, Mir Jafar, Siraj-ud-Daulah, Shah Alam II.
