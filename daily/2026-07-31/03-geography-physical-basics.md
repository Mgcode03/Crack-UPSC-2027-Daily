# Day 1 Part 3: Physical Geography Basics – Earth and India (Read Time ~60 min)

## Why Physical Geography
Geography questions (10-18 per year in Prelims) test conceptual understanding of physical features, climate, resources. Mains GS-I has geography of India and world. Build strong fundamentals first.

## Shape and Size of Earth
Earth is an oblate spheroid – flattened at poles, bulging at equator. Equatorial diameter is greater than polar diameter. This causes variation in gravity and affects climate and ocean currents.

Latitude: Angular distance north or south of equator (0° to 90°). Parallel lines. Important latitudes: Equator (0°), Tropic of Cancer (23½°N), Tropic of Capricorn (23½°S), Arctic Circle (66½°N), Antarctic Circle (66½°S).

Longitude: Angular distance east or west of Prime Meridian (0° at Greenwich). Converge at poles. Used for time calculation. 15° longitude = 1 hour time difference. Indian Standard Time is based on 82½°E (near Allahabad).

## Structure of Earth
Crust: Outermost thin layer. Continental crust thicker (granitic), oceanic thinner (basaltic).
Mantle: Below crust, semi-solid, convection currents cause plate movements.
Core: Outer core liquid (responsible for magnetic field), inner core solid (iron-nickel).

The boundary between crust and mantle is Mohorovičić discontinuity (Moho). Between mantle and core is Gutenberg discontinuity.

## Plate Tectonics
Earth’s lithosphere is divided into tectonic plates that move over the asthenosphere. Types of plate boundaries:

- Divergent: Plates move apart – mid-ocean ridges, rift valleys (e.g., Mid-Atlantic Ridge, East African Rift).
- Convergent: Plates collide – fold mountains (Himalayas – Indian plate under Eurasian), trenches, volcanoes.
- Transform: Plates slide past each other – earthquakes (San Andreas Fault).

Indian plate is moving northward, colliding with Eurasian plate – formation of Himalayas and ongoing seismic activity.

## Rocks and Minerals
Igneous: Formed from cooling magma/lava. Intrusive (granite), Extrusive (basalt).
Sedimentary: Formed by deposition and compaction (sandstone, limestone, shale).
Metamorphic: Changed by heat and pressure (marble from limestone, slate from shale, gneiss from granite).

Rock cycle: Continuous transformation among the three types.

## Earthquakes and Volcanoes
Earthquake: Sudden release of energy along faults. Measured by Richter scale (magnitude) and Mercalli scale (intensity). Focus is the point of origin inside earth; epicentre is the point on surface above focus.

India’s seismic zones: Zone V (highest – Northeast, Kashmir, Kutch), Zone IV, III, II.

Volcanoes: Opening through which magma reaches surface. Types – shield, composite, cinder cone. Ring of Fire around Pacific is most active. India has Barren Island (Andaman) – only active volcano.

## Indian Physiography – Broad Divisions
1. The Northern Mountains (Himalayas): Young fold mountains. Parallel ranges – Himadri (Greater), Himachal (Lesser), Shiwalik (Outer). Important passes: Khyber, Bolan, Shipki La, Nathu La, Bomdi La.
2. The Northern Plains: Formed by Indus, Ganga, Brahmaputra and their tributaries. Alluvial soil, fertile. Divided into Bhabar, Terai, Bhangar, Khadar.
3. The Peninsular Plateau: Oldest landmass. Deccan Plateau, Central Highlands, Northeastern Plateau. Made of igneous and metamorphic rocks. Western Ghats and Eastern Ghats.
4. The Indian Desert (Thar): Arid, sandy, low rainfall. Located in Rajasthan.
5. The Coastal Plains: Western (Konkan, Kannad, Malabar) – narrower; Eastern (Northern Circars, Coromandel) – wider, deltas of Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna, Cauvery.
6. The Islands: Andaman & Nicobar (Bay of Bengal – volcanic and tectonic), Lakshadweep (Arabian Sea – coral).

## Drainage Systems
Himalayan rivers: Perennial, snow-fed + rain-fed. Indus, Ganga, Brahmaputra systems. Form deep gorges and large plains.

Peninsular rivers: Rain-fed, seasonal. East-flowing (Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna, Cauvery – form deltas), West-flowing (Narmada, Tapi – form estuaries).

Key fact: Narmada and Tapi flow in rift valleys.

## Climate Basics for India
India has tropical monsoon climate. Factors: Latitude, Himalayas (barrier to cold winds), Thar Desert, Indian Ocean, jet streams, El Niño/La Niña.

Seasons: Winter (Dec-Feb), Summer (Mar-May), Southwest Monsoon (Jun-Sep), Retreating Monsoon (Oct-Nov).

Monsoon mechanism: Differential heating of land and sea, ITCZ shift, Coriolis force, jet streams.

Memorise: Tropic of Cancer passes through Gujarat, Rajasthan, MP, Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, West Bengal, Tripura, Mizoram.

## Key Facts to Memorise
- IST: 82°30'E
- Highest peak in India: Kangchenjunga (not K2 which is in PoK)
- Longest river in India: Ganga
- Largest drainage basin: Ganga
- Western Ghats higher and continuous than Eastern Ghats
- Only active volcano: Barren Island
