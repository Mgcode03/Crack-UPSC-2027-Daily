## CSAT – Why You Cannot Ignore It
CSAT is qualifying (33% i.e. about 67 marks out of 200). Many candidates fail here despite clearing GS. It tests comprehension, reasoning, basic numeracy and decision making. Practice daily from now so it never becomes a bottleneck.

### Comprehension Strategy
Read the passage carefully once. Identify the main idea, tone (positive/negative/neutral), and what the author is arguing. Questions usually ask: inference, central idea, meaning of a phrase in context, strengthen/weaken argument. Do not bring outside knowledge. Stick to the passage.

Practice tip: After reading a paragraph, summarise it in one sentence in your mind.

### Logical Reasoning Basics
- Statements and Conclusions: Check if conclusion necessarily follows.
- Statements and Assumptions: Assumption is unstated but necessary for the statement to hold.
- Cause and Effect: Identify which is cause, which is effect, or if independent.
- Course of Action: Practical, feasible, and directly related to the problem.
- Syllogism: All A are B, Some B are C → conclusions about A and C. Use Venn diagrams mentally.

### Basic Numeracy (Class X level)
Number system, percentages, ratio-proportion, averages, simple and compound interest, time-speed-distance, time and work, profit-loss, data interpretation (tables, bar graphs, pie charts, line graphs).

Key formulae to memorise:
- Percentage change = (Change / Original) × 100
- Simple Interest = (P × R × T)/100
- Compound Interest = P(1 + R/100)^n – P
- Average = Sum / Number of observations
- Speed = Distance / Time

Work daily on 5-10 questions of mixed type. Accuracy matters more than speed initially.

## Ethics, Integrity and Aptitude – First Glimpse (GS-IV)
GS-IV tests your attitude, not just knowledge. It has theory + case studies.

Core concepts starting today:

**Ethics**: Set of moral principles that guide behaviour. It is about what is right and wrong. Sources: religion, philosophy, family, society, Constitution, laws.

**Integrity**: Consistency between thoughts, words and actions. Honesty + moral uprightness. Public servants must have integrity to maintain public trust.

**Aptitude**: Natural ability or talent for a particular skill. In civil services context – administrative aptitude, emotional intelligence, problem-solving aptitude.

**Attitude**: Predisposition to respond positively or negatively to a person, object or idea. Components: cognitive (beliefs), affective (feelings), behavioural (actions).

**Emotional Intelligence**: Ability to recognise, understand and manage one’s own emotions and those of others. Important for decision making under stress and public dealing.

**Values**: Enduring beliefs about what is good or desirable. Constitutional values: justice, liberty, equality, fraternity, secularism, democracy.

In case studies, always structure answer as: Stakeholders involved → Ethical dilemma → Options available → Evaluation of options on ethical principles → Recommended course of action with justification → Long-term measures.

Memorise the definition of integrity and emotional intelligence. They appear frequently.
